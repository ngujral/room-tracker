FHSA contribution room for 2026

A First Home Savings Account gives $8,000 of room a year, up to $40,000 over your lifetime. Contributions for 2026 must be made by December 31, 2026 — unlike an RRSP, there’s no 60-day grace period.

How FHSA room works

  • Room starts when you open the account, not when you turn 18. Opening one early — even with $0 in it — starts the clock.
  • Up to $8,000 of unused room carries forward to the next year. So the most you can put in during a single year is $16,000.
  • Withdrawals don’t give room back.
  • Over-contributing costs 1% a month on the excess, the same as a TFSA.

Who can open an FHSA

You must be a Canadian resident, between 18 and 71, and a first-time home buyer.

Why it’s the best account for a down payment

The FHSA is the only registered account that’s tax-deductible going in and tax-free coming out for a qualifying first home. An RRSP is deductible but taxed on withdrawal; a TFSA is tax-free on withdrawal but not deductible.

You can also combine an FHSA withdrawal with the RRSP Home Buyers’ Plan (up to $60,000) for the same home.

What if you never buy a home?

The account can stay open for up to 15 years, or until the end of the year you turn 71, whichever comes first. If you don’t buy, you can transfer the money to your RRSP or RRIF tax-free — and it doesn’t use any of your RRSP room.

Common questions

How much can I put in an FHSA in 2026?

$8,000, plus up to $8,000 of unused room carried forward from 2025 — so up to $16,000 if you opened the account in 2025 or earlier and didn’t contribute.

What is the FHSA lifetime limit?

$40,000.

What is the FHSA deadline for 2026?

December 31, 2026. FHSA contributions don’t get the 60-day grace period RRSPs have.

Do FHSA withdrawals give room back?

No. Once contributed, that room is used even if you withdraw.

What happens to my FHSA if I don’t buy a home?

After up to 15 years (or by the year you turn 71), you can move it to an RRSP or RRIF tax-free without using RRSP room.
