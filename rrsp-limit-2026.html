RRSP contribution limit for 2026

Your 2026 RRSP limit is 18% of your 2025 earned income, up to $33,810, plus any unused room from earlier years. The deadline to contribute for the 2026 tax year is March 1, 2027.

Where to find your exact RRSP limit

Your personal limit — called your RRSP deduction limit — is printed on your Notice of Assessment from your last tax return, and in CRA My Account under RRSP. It already includes unused room carried forward and subtracts any pension adjustment from a workplace pension.

Which contributions count toward 2026

Contributions made from March 3, 2026 to March 1, 2027 can be deducted on your 2026 return. Contributions made in the first 60 days of 2026 (January 1 to March 2) could be claimed on your 2025 return instead — those are usually already reflected in your Notice of Assessment.

The $2,000 over-contribution buffer

Unlike a TFSA, an RRSP lets you go up to $2,000 over your limit without penalty. You can’t deduct that $2,000, but it can grow tax-sheltered. Past the buffer, CRA charges 1% a month on the excess.

Spousal RRSPs

Contributions to a spousal RRSP use your room, while the money belongs to your spouse in retirement — a common way to even out retirement income between partners.

Using your RRSP to buy a first home

The Home Buyers’ Plan lets you withdraw up to $60,000 from your RRSP for a qualifying first home, repaid over 15 years. You can combine it with a First Home Savings Account withdrawal for the same home.

When an RRSP must end

You must convert your RRSP to a RRIF (or an annuity) by December 31 of the year you turn 71.

Common questions

What is the RRSP limit for 2026?

18% of your 2025 earned income, up to a maximum of $33,810, plus unused room from earlier years and minus any pension adjustment.

What is the RRSP deadline for the 2026 tax year?

March 1, 2027 — 60 days after the end of 2026.

What is the RRSP limit for 2027?

The 2027 dollar maximum is $35,390.

Can I go over my RRSP limit?

Yes, by up to $2,000 without penalty, though that amount isn’t deductible. Beyond $2,000, the excess is taxed at 1% a month.

How much can I take out under the Home Buyers’ Plan?

Up to $60,000 from your RRSP for a qualifying first home, repaid over 15 years.
